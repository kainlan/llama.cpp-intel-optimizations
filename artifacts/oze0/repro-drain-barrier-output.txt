QUESTION: ggml_sycl_graph_boundary_reset_arenas() relies on
drain_retained_handles(true) to make "the SCRATCH zone is empty" a safe
precondition for graph launch. Is that drain a barrier against another
context that owns scratch?

12 of 12 completed runs reproduce, at 79-99% of boundary crossings. The three
non-results are the harness timeout (the boundary thread spins millions of
drains per run under host load ~55); no run has ever reported 0 with a
faithful hold window. An earlier variant with a 200-cycle hold window -- far
shorter than a real stream_dma submission -- reproduced in only 1 of 5 runs,
which is why the window was widened to something honest rather than left where
it flattered the barrier.

WHAT THIS MEANS

drain_retained_handles(true) waits for state.queue to empty and state.active to
reach 0. A thread holding an allocation that has not YET been handed to
retain_handles_until_event() is in neither structure, so it is invisible. The
drain therefore returns "all clear" while another context demonstrably owns
scratch, and the reset that follows is correctly refused -- aborting graph
launch.

CONSEQUENCE FOR ATTRIBUTION

This predicts exactly ONE live allocation in the live-zone dump: a single
concurrent owner caught in the window, not an accumulating set. That matches
the measurement on the merge commit (alloc_id=226, one allocation,
available=511.2 MB vs target=512.0 MB -- a shortfall of 828504 bytes, i.e.
exactly one). It does NOT match the "accumulation" reading in cf802823c's
commit message, which is retracted; see the correction commit.

CONSEQUENCE FOR THE EPIC (llama.cpp-iiff)

The reset cannot be made safe by draining harder, ordering better, or holding a
lock longer -- the owner is not observable from the boundary at all. This is
positive evidence for Phase 2's "delete the reset" rather than for any attempt
to repair the precondition.
